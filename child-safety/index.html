Child Safety Standards

Effective 2026-09-24

Showup is a private app for groups of friends to plan and check in to real-world sessions. We are committed to keeping children safe, and these are the standards we hold Showup to.

Showup is for adults only

Showup is for people aged 18 and over. You must be at least 18 to use it, as set out in our Terms of Use and Privacy Policy. Our Terms also forbid posting Session Photos of anyone under 18.

Zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation

We have zero tolerance for child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and for child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Showup does not allow sexual, exploitative or illegal content, harassment, or abusive users. We will remove such content, suspend or remove the accounts responsible, and cooperate with law enforcement where required by law.

How Showup limits risk

  • Private by design: Crews are private groups of up to 12 members, joined by invite. There are no public profiles and no public feed.
  • Private photos: Session Photos are stored privately and shown only to Crew members, through short-lived links.
  • Invite links: someone with an invite link sees only the Crew name, the member count and a short summary of the next Plan.

Report and block in the app

  • Block: block anyone in your Crews from the Crew's settings.
  • Report a member: report a member from the Crew's settings and choose a reason.
  • Report a Session Photo: report a Session Photo from the photo viewer (sexual content is one of the reasons you can choose).

We don't tell anyone when you block or report them, and reports are never shown to other users. Reports are reviewed, and we take appropriate action, such as removing content or suspending an account.

How to report a concern

Use the in-app report and block tools above, or email us at business@afrozbuilds.online. Include what happened and the Crew or account involved. Please don't send us any sexual or exploitative images; describe the content instead.

If a child is in immediate danger, contact your local emergency services first. You can also report child sexual abuse material to your national authority, such as the Internet Watch Foundation or the NCMEC CyberTipline.

Contact
